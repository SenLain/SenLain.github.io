The third Tech&Meet I attended was completely different from the first and second one. This time, the topic was "The Birth of NATO's Cyber Defense" and the speaker was Martin De Pauw. I have to say, this session was incredibly interesting to me. I was concentrated from start to finish, which does not always happen during guest lectures.

## The Speaker

Martin De Pauw was a great storyteller. He did not just throw facts and dates at us. Instead, he gave a very engaging insight into his career and his time working with NATO. Hearing his personal perspective made the whole topic feel much more real. You could tell he had actually lived through the events he was describing.

## What Surprised Me The Most

The thing that surprised me the most was the timeline. NATO only recognised cyberspace as a dedicated domain for defense in 2016. That felt incredibly late to me. For such a big and powerful alliance, I assumed cybersecurity would have been a priority much earlier. It made me realise that even the largest organisations can be slow to adapt to new threats.

Martin explained that cyber used to be seen as something only for technical experts. But over time, NATO realised that cyber has no borders. It affects everyone, not just the military or IT departments. Today, cyber defense is considered just as important as land, sea, and air.

The session also included some history about NATO itself. Martin walked us through how the alliance has evolved over time. It was helpful to understand the bigger picture before diving into the cyber aspect.

## My Takeaway

This session really stuck with me. I walked away with a much better understanding of why cybersecurity is so crucial in today's world. It is not just about protecting computers anymore. It is about protecting nations, infrastructure, and people.

I am glad I attended this one. It was easily my favourite Tech&Meet so far.
